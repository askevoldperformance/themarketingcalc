The Ultimate Guide to CTR: Meaning, Formula, and How to Improve It

Robin Askevold Performance Marketing Specialist Updated August 03, 2026

You can show your ad or page to millions of people, but if nobody clicks, the campaign falls flat. CTR is the metric that tells you how well your messaging actually connects with the people seeing it - and it is one of the most actionable numbers in digital marketing.

What is CTR? Meaning and definition

CTR stands for Click-Through Rate. It measures the ratio of users who click on a link to the total number of users who see it - whether that is an ad, a search result, an email, or a banner. Your CTR tells you how compelling and relevant your content is to the audience it reaches.

High CTR indicates that your messaging resonates with your audience and matches their intent. Low CTR suggests a disconnect between what users are looking for and what your ad or link offers - or that your creative elements need work.

How to calculate CTR

Divide the number of clicks by the total number of impressions, then multiply by 100 to get a percentage.

CTR formula
CTR = (Total Clicks ÷ Total Impressions) × 100

Step-by-step calculation example

Say you are running a Google Search ad campaign. Your ad is shown 50,000 times and receives 1,500 clicks.

1
Divide total clicks by total impressions
1,500 ÷ 50,000 = 0.03
2
Multiply by 100 to get a percentage
0.03 × 100 = 3%
Your CTR is 3% - meaning 3 out of every 100 people who saw the ad clicked through to your landing page.

What is a good click-through rate?

There is no single ideal CTR - averages vary significantly by channel, industry, and placement. Here are the benchmarks worth knowing:

ⓘ
Display banner ads
The first ever banner ad in 1994 had a 44% CTR. Today, due to banner blindness, the average display ad CTR sits around 0.1% to 0.5%.
ⓘ
Google Search ads (PPC)
Because search ads appear when someone is actively looking for a solution, they benefit from far higher engagement - typically averaging 3% to 5% or higher for well-optimised campaigns.
ⓘ
Email marketing
Email CTR measures how many recipients clicked a link in your email out of total delivered messages. A solid email CTR generally tracks between 2% and 4%.

CTR benchmarks only matter relative to your channel and objective. A 0.3% display CTR can be excellent if your CPM is low and your landing page converts well. A 2% search CTR may be underperforming if your competitors are at 6%.

CTR and its relationship to CPC and Quality Score

In Google Ads, CTR is not just a vanity metric - it directly affects how much you pay per click. Google's Quality Score algorithm rewards ads with high CTR by lowering your Cost Per Click (CPC). This means a well-optimised ad with a high CTR can achieve better ad positions at a lower cost than a competitor bidding more but with weaker creative.

The relationship works in both directions: a low CTR signals to Google that your ad is not relevant to the queries it is serving, which raises your CPC and can reduce your impression share over time.

The impact of AI on organic CTR

The search landscape has shifted significantly with the rollout of AI-generated search summaries - Google AI Overviews and similar features. Recent industry data shows that organic CTR for top-ranking positions has dropped by as much as 30 to 35% for non-branded, informational keywords. Because the AI answers the user's query directly on the results page, users have less reason to click through to external websites.

To adapt, modern SEO focuses on optimising for snippet features, using schema markup, and targeting high-intent transactional keywords where users still need to click to take action - product pages, booking flows, tools, and calculators rather than purely informational content.

How to improve your CTR

Write stronger headlines. Your headline is the single biggest lever for improving CTR in both paid and organic search. Include the keyword, lead with the benefit, and match the language your audience uses when they search.

Use clear calls to action. Vague CTAs like "Learn More" consistently underperform specific ones like "Calculate Your CPM" or "See Pricing." Tell users exactly what happens when they click.

Match ad copy to search intent. An ad that mirrors the exact language of the query converts significantly better than a generic brand message. Use keyword insertion where relevant and ensure your headline directly addresses what the user is looking for.

Test ad extensions. In Google Ads, sitelinks, callouts, and structured snippets increase the visual footprint of your ad and add more clickable surface area - both of which improve CTR without changing your core message.

Optimise your meta descriptions for organic search. While meta descriptions are not a direct ranking factor, they appear in the search snippet and significantly influence whether a user clicks. Write them to address the searcher's intent and include a reason to click over the other results on the page.

CTR compared to other key metrics

CTR measures interest. To understand the full value of a campaign, it needs to be read alongside conversion rate and CPM.

Metric Focus area What it tells you How to improve it

CPM Reach and cost How much you are paying per 1,000 impressions Broaden audience, improve ad quality, test formats

Frequently asked questions

A good CTR for Google Search ads is generally 3% to 5% for most industries, with well-optimised campaigns in high-intent categories often exceeding 6% to 8%. Display ads have much lower benchmarks - 0.1% to 0.5% is typical. The right benchmark depends on your industry and campaign type. What matters more than the raw number is whether your CTR is improving over time and whether it is translating into conversions at a profitable CPC.
Google has stated that organic CTR is not a direct ranking signal in its core algorithm. However, CTR heavily influences Google Ads Quality Score, which directly affects your CPC and ad position in paid search. For organic SEO, high CTR is correlated with strong rankings because pages that rank well typically have well-written titles and descriptions that attract clicks - but the causality runs in both directions.
CTR measures how many people clicked on your ad or link out of everyone who saw it. Conversion rate measures how many people who clicked then completed a desired action - a purchase, a sign-up, or a lead form. You can have a very high CTR and a low conversion rate if your landing page does not deliver on the promise of your ad. Both metrics need to be optimised together for a profitable campaign.
The most common causes of organic CTR drops are the introduction of AI Overviews or featured snippets above your result, a ranking position drop pushing your result further down the page, a change in the search result format (images, shopping ads, or maps taking up more space), or seasonal changes in search behaviour. Check Google Search Console to identify which pages and queries have seen the biggest CTR drop and correlate with any algorithm update dates.
Email open rate measures how many recipients opened your email. Email CTR measures how many recipients clicked a link inside the email, usually expressed as a percentage of total delivered emails. CTR is generally considered the more meaningful metric because it measures actual engagement with your content rather than just whether the subject line was compelling enough to open. A typical email CTR is 2% to 4% for B2C and 3% to 6% for B2B.
In Google Ads, CTR is one of the key components of Quality Score. A higher CTR signals to Google that your ad is relevant to the query, which improves your Quality Score. A higher Quality Score lowers your required bid to achieve the same ad position - meaning you pay less per click than a competitor with a lower Quality Score bidding the same amount. Improving CTR is one of the most efficient ways to reduce CPC without reducing bids.
